# Travel Encounters

Whenever an encounter check indicates there should be a random encounter while the PCs are traveling or resting outdoors, you can roll on the Travel Encounters table. Each encounter is further detailed below in alphabetical order.

When you roll for an encounter, it can happen at any point in the travel day.

## Bad Weather

The bad weather event lasts 24 hours and depends on the habitat where the weather occurs, or the season when it happens if the PCs are in a temperate climate, as shown on the Bad Weather table. Each climate or season has two events on the table. Roll any die. On an odd result, use the first listed event. On an even one, use the second.

### Bad Weather

| Climate or Season | Weather |
| --- | --- |
| Cold or Winter | Blizzard or Cold Snap |
| Desert | Heat Wave or Sandstorm |
| Fall | Rain or Thunderstorm |
| Spring | Rain or Thunderstorm |
| Tropical or Summer | Heat Wave or Thunderstorm |

### Blizzard

During a blizzard, the group’s daily travel speed is reduced by 2 hexes. At the end of each hour a human spends outside without cold weather gear, they make a Strength RR:

**2d10 + S**
- **≤11 (Tier 1):** They take 4d6 P dam.
- **12–16 (Tier 2):** They take 3d6 P dam.
- **17+ (Tier 3):** They take 2d6 P dam.

A creature takes a cumulative -2 penalty to this test for each time they’ve previously made this test before donning cold weather gear, taking shelter, or experiencing a change in the weather.

Guides, scouts, and trackers take a double bane on tests made to fulfill their roles during the blizzard.

### Cold Snap

During a cold snap, at the end of each hour a human spends outside without cold weather gear, they make a Strength RR:

**2d10 + S**
- **≤11 (Tier 1):** They take 3d6 P dam.
- **12–16 (Tier 2):** They take 2d6 P dam.
- **17+ (Tier 3):** They take 1d6 P dam.

A creature takes a cumulative -2 penalty to this test for each time they previously made this test before donning cold weather gear, taking shelter, or experiencing a change in the weather.

### Heat Wave

During a heat wave, if a creature travels more than 2 hexes in a day, they must make a Strength RR:

**2d10 + S**
- **≤11 (Tier 1):** They take 2d6 P dam.
- **12–16 (Tier 2):** They take 1d6 P dam.
- **17+ (Tier 3):** No effect.

### Rain

In the rain, the group’s travel speed is reduced by 1 hex. Creatures carrying rations must make the following Mind test:

**2d10 + M**
- **≤11 (Tier 1):** They lose 1d6 + 1 rations to mold.
- **12–16 (Tier 2):** They lose 1 ration to mold.
- **17+ (Tier 3):** No effect.

### Sandstorm

During a sandstorm, the group’s travel speed is reduced by 2 hexes. Guides, scouts, and trackers take a double bane on tests made to fulfill their roles during a sandstorm.

### Thunderstorm

In a thunderstorm, the group’s travel pace is reduced by 2 hexes. Creatures carrying rations must make the following Mind test:

**2d10 + M**
- **≤11 (Tier 1):** They lose 2d6 rations to mold.
- **12–16 (Tier 2):** They lose 1d6 rations to mold.
- **17+ (Tier 3):** No effect.

Guides, scouts, and trackers take a bane on tests made to fulfill their roles during the thunderstorm.

## Miasma-Touched

Humans who are permanently affected by the Miasma are wicked, but that doesn’t mean they immediately resort to violence when they encounter the PCs. These humans form bonds with others and are capable of showing kindness if they can get something for it. They don’t have a death wish, and therefore don’t seek death. Roll 1d6 times on the Miasma-Touched Humans table to determine the humans the PCs meet. Then roll on the Miasma-Touched Encounters table to determine their reaction to the PCs.

The reactions on the table are prompts, meant to inspire different encounters with Miasma-touched humans so that not every run-in is the same. You can spice things up by putting your own spin on the prompts. A group of bandits might actually be looking for capable recruits instead of treasure. Cultists might have an artifact stashed nearby the PCs can look for. A cannibal might have a refined palate and eat only nobles. The encounter descriptions aren’t handcuffs. Let them inspire you and change up whatever you like.

## Strong Miasma

If the PCs don’t travel in any hexes affected by the Miasma today thanks to an upgraded beacon institution (see Village in *Characters*), then they don’t experience an encounter.

The Miasma the PCs travel and rest in gets stronger for 24 hours. Every PC takes a bane on RRs made to resist the Miasma’s effects and the translucent haze becomes an opaque fog. While this effect lasts, guides, scouts, and trackers take a bane on tests related to their travel role.

If the PCs are lost when this encounter occurs or get lost during it, they are magically transported to a hex that is 1d10 hexes away in a direction of the Ref’s choice.

## Traveler

The PCs encounter 1d10 other humans traveling on the road. The stat blocks for those humans can be rolled on the Travelers table. Then roll on the Traveler Encounters table to determine what makes the encounter noteworthy.

The encounters on the table are prompts, meant to inspire different encounters with traveling humans so that not every run-in is the same. You can spice things up by putting your own spin on the prompts. A group of humans who are lost might have a few Miasma-touched among them who cause trouble. A group hunting what they believe is an animal could be tracking a deadly monster instead. A group of people searching for treasure might want something the PCs already have. The encounter descriptions aren’t handcuffs. Let them inspire you and change up whatever you like.

### Traveler Reactions

Unless the travelers are in dire straits, they approach others on the road with caution. After all, many of the humans outside the villages are Miasma-touched. These travelers keep their guard up until the PCs say or do something that shows they’re kind.

## Wild Animal

Roll for the animal encounter on the appropriate habitat table where the encounter occurs. Then roll an attitude on the Wild Animal Reaction table to determine how they react to the PCs. Some attitude descriptions on the table refer to others, indicated by underlined text.

When the PCs meet a group of animals, it is up to you if all animals have the same attitude or if one, some, or all animals in the group have an individual reaction.

The attitudes on the table are prompts, meant to inspire different encounters with animals so that not every run-in is the same. You can spice things up by putting your own spin on the prompts. An asleep animal might be laying on top of something valuable. A treasure-seeking animal might have a hoard of shinies in a nest nearby the PCs can search for. An injured animal might also be territorial, and relax and allow PCs into their territory if their injuries are healed. A protective animal who is also hungry might get distracted from guarding their nest if given food. The attitudes aren’t handcuffs. Let them inspire you and change up whatever you like.

# Creature Stats

Most creatures have the same stats as PCs with the following exceptions.

*(Playtest Note: The current list of monsters includes just what is needed for the playtest. More creatures will be in future tests.)*

## Slots

Monsters don’t have slots and die when they are reduced to 0 Stamina. Humans and animals have the potential to be allies, so they do have slots, which count as backpack slots for them.

If a PC or other creature with backpack slots gains the stats of a different creature, they keep their original number of backpack slots.

## Power

A creature’s power signifies their general toughness and threat to other creatures. In this booklet, a creature’s power is scaled from 0 to 50, though future products could go even higher! The greater the power, the more deadly head-to-head combat is with the creature. The more experienced the PCs are, the more likely they are to survive an encounter with a creature of high power. Starting PCs stand no chance in a head-to-head battle against creatures of power 11 or greater (and even creatures of power 1 or more in groups can really mess them up).

## Reactions

Like PCs, most creatures have only 1 reaction each round, but some have more. If a creature can use more than one reaction each round, their stat block will say so.

## X/Rest

Some creatures have a feature that has “X/Rest” written next to it. The creature can only use this feature X number of times between rests.

If a creature who has expended 1 or more uses of an X/Rest feature rolls a crit, they can regain 1 use of that feature as the crit’s benefit.

## Attacks That Grab

Some attacks can result in other creatures being grabbed. If a creature is grabbing the maximum number of creatures they can have grabbed and then uses such an attack to attack one or more targets who are not grabbed by them, the attacker must release a number of creatures they have grabbed equal to the number of ungrabbed targets to make the attack.

# Animals

Animals can be friend and foe alike to crows. Some are pets (see *Characters*). Others are wild predators who see the PCs as their next meal.

## Potential Pets

All animals are potential pets and therefore have slots, but not all of these animals are sold as pets at the village stables. Some you have to find and befriend in the wild.

If a PC turns into an animal through the use of magic, add the animal’s slots to their Stamina total while in the animal form instead of giving the PC more slots.

## Wild Animals

Wild animals walk the land of Cornath, searching for food, shelter, and mates. When you want to create a wild animal encounter, you can roll to determine what kind and how many animals the PCs encounter on the appropriate animal habitat encounter table.

### Ending the Fight

Animals rarely fight to the death. Most flee when it’s clear to them that they can’t win a fight (typically if their Stamina is at 0), or if they get what they want. A threatened animal stops fighting when the threat runs off, hungry animals stop fighting if they get something (or someone) to eat, cornered animals flee if given an opening or submit if they can’t escape and are near death.

Hungry predators want to expend the least amount of energy possible to get their meal. If the PCs hide or take cover in a hard-to-reach place or surround themselves with traps that harm the animal, there’s a good chance they’ll eventually run off in search of an easier meal.

# Humans

Humans are the other people of Cornath that interact with the PCs. Hirelings, bandits, cultists, and other NPCs use the human stat blocks. For more information on hirelings, see *Characters*. Many humans the PCs interact with don’t need stat blocks, but if they do, you can choose the most appropriate one from below.

Unless otherwise noted, each human has only 1 use of a listed expertise.

## Ending the Fight

As sapient creatures, most humans flee or surrender when they’re clearly outmatched. Even most humans with souls warped by the Miasma still have a logical part of their brain that says, “Get out of this and live to fight another day.”

Most humans have a reason for fighting another group. They are defending themselves, their loved ones, or their property, robbing the other person, or have some emotional desire to do harm (though perhaps not murder). Once that desire is fulfilled, they stop fighting. A human defending their home doesn’t pursue a fleeing threat, and a bandit robbing a passerby stops stabbing once they have a full purse.

A human may also stop fighting if someone or something they love is threatened, or if presented with a desirable quid pro quo.

When a human fighting alone drops to 0 Stamina, they flee, unless the Ref deems otherwise. When a group of humans battling the PCs has their number halved, they flee, unless the Ref deems otherwise.

# Monsters

The term “monster” is used in this game to refer to creatures who are not humans or animals. There are six monster types in the core game:

- **Angels:** Though called angels for their otherworldly home and beauty, these creatures are far from heavenly. They radiate magic that charms and warps the minds of their prey.
- **Blood Creatures:** Amalgamations of flesh and fluid of humans and animals, blood creatures are held together by the same magic that makes them ravenous for the flesh of other creatures. Most are animalistic in nature.
- **Demons:** Beasts summoned from the pits of Hell, demons slaughter other creatures for fun. They’re fast, strong, and tenacious.
- **Plants:** Animated by necromancy, these plants use viney tendrils and roots to move, stab, and choke prey to quench a literal bloodthirst.
- **Undead:** The reanimated remains of humans, twisted into killing machines that abhor anything alive.
- **Uniques:** Some monsters in this book are unique experiments of the necromancers. Most have a level of sapience the other monsters lack and there exists only one of each such creature in Cornath.

## Monster Names

Except for uniques, each monster doesn’t have a specific name. Instead, a monster is given a letter after its type for referencing in the rules, like “blood creature A” or “undead C.” The arrival of monsters threw the world into chaos, so there is no universal nomenclature for them.

Each monster has art for you to show the players, so they can come up with names for them. There’s a list of colloquial names that NPCs might use to refer to a monster so the Ref doesn’t have to come up with them on the fly (but they still can).

## Hates and Likes

Every monster type has likes and hates: objects, other creatures, sights, smells, and sounds that can be used to attract, distract, and misdirect them. They can be used to bait traps and ambushes or allow PCs to sneak by or flee from a threat that’s beyond their capabilities.

### Likes

When a monster encounters a hint of something they like, they seek out the source and investigate further. If they find something they like and no danger is apparent, the monster spends time interacting with it.

If several monsters stumble upon a small amount of something they like, they could fight over which of them gets to keep it.

> [!important] Universal Like
> All monsters like living, vulnerable prey and the distress sounds created by animals and humans.
> 
> Above all, monsters like living, so they are unlikely to engage with a like while confronted with obvious danger.

### Hates

When a monster encounters something they hate, they hunt down the source and try to destroy it. If a weaker monster (with a power of 9 or lower) finds an overwhelming amount of something they hate, they might flee instead. If a monster hates cheese, they might tear a wheel apart, but would flee from an entire cavern full of dairy delights.

Above all, monsters hate dying, so they are unlikely to engage with a hate while a bunch of crows attack them; however, if one of the PCs is wearing or carrying something they hate, they prioritize attacking that PC.

## No Darkness Penalty

Monsters have special senses that mean darkness and dim light impose no penalty on them.

## Suspicious Circumstances

Monsters can be suspicious of finding something they like or hate in an unusual or dangerous place, such as their favorite food suddenly appearing in the middle of a room or an object they hate hanging over a lava pit.

If a monster approaches something they like or hate in an unusual or dangerous situation, you can have them make this test to see how they react:

**2d10 + M**
- **≤11 (Tier 1):** The monster approaches the like or hate without suspicion.
- **12–16 (Tier 2):** The monster approaches with suspicion, investigating anything that seems out of place before approaching the like or hate.
- **17+ (Tier 3):** The monster doesn’t approach the like or hate, and leaves the area but stays close, waiting to find out what’s really going on and preparing an ambush for potential danger or gathering allies to better investigate.

## Ending the Fight

Monsters are more aggressive than most humans or animals, but they’re still likely to flee from a fight when it becomes clear they’re going to lose. Weaker monsters tend to stick with others of their ilk, and never stray too far from that pack to pursue prey. Stronger monsters pursue prey on their own if they think they can win the fight.

Monsters in dungeons rarely pursue prey far outside the walls of their home, remaining in their territory to defend it. Monsters wandering the wilds are vulnerable, and tend to flee earlier if a battle starts going south to find safety and a way to recover from their wounds.

Monsters will often allow the PCs time to flee after killing a human or pet, provided their kill is large enough to satiate their hunger.

## Blood Creatures

Craelin created blood creatures—amalgamations of fluid and flesh of several creatures brought together in the form of an entirely new predator. Blood creatures have exposed musculature and red features. They know on some instinctual level that their existence is unnatural, and this knowledge drives them to feast on the flesh of humans and animals as a way to prove they are superior and belong in the world.

### Likes
Blood creatures like **large quantities of animal or human blood (a gallon or more), the scent of iron,** and **the sound of dripping liquid**.

### Hates
Blood creatures hate **dry bones without any flesh, containers holding large quantities of clean freshwater (a gallon or more),** and **soap**.

## Undead

Warped and reanimated by the magic of Ornassa, undead despise the living. Made from the remains of dead humans, some part of even the simplest undead remembers the previous existence of all the humans who make up their living parts, though vaguely. If one can tap into the most personal of memories for an undead, they just might cause the creature’s overwhelming hatred of the living to falter for a moment.

### Likes
Undead like **images, sounds, and smells that could remind them of the people they loved most in life, animal and human corpses that have been dead less than 24 hours, cold temperatures,** and **the scent of wet earth**.

### Hates
Undead hate **images, sounds, and smells that could remind them of the people they hated most in life, iconography of the gods, a fire the size of a bonfire or larger,** and **stained glass art**.
