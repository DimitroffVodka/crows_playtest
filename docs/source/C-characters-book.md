# It’s a Living

It also might be a dying, but you knew the risks when you became a crow. This book contains the rules for players to create and advance their crows, their equipment, and their village. Options here refer to rules in *The Rules* booklet.

# Crow Creation

To create a crow, follow these steps.

## 1. Roll For Background

You weren’t always a crow. Your background informs the statistics and equipment you bring with you when you first enter the profession. To get a background, roll 2d6 on the Backgrounds table. The first die result is the left d6 column of the table and the second is the right d6 column.

## 2. Record Statistics

Record your background’s statistics on a piece of paper with a pencil. Each background gives you 1 use in some expertises, a Stamina score, and a starting trait from a trait tree (a full description of each trait is found in [[#Traits]]). All PCs have a starting speed of 5.

### Characteristic Adjustments

Your background makes one of your characteristics a 2. Sometimes the background assigns this increase. Other times it gives you a choice. Your remaining characteristics can be your choice of a 1 and a 0 or a -1 and a 2, assigned however you like.

### Name and Feature

Give your PC a name and a distinguishing feature. This could be a tattoo, distinct body odor, unique voice, strong personality trait, or the like. Just give them one thing to stand out.

## 3. Equipment Cards

Your background also gives you a set of starting equipment. **Every PC has an empty coin purse, a knife, a rope, six rations, and 3d6 gc.**

## 4. Village Stuff

Make an NPC connection who lives in your village. If this is your group’s first time making PCs for a campaign, work with other players to make a village and name it (see [[#The Village]]).

# Advancement

Whenever you recover treasure or equipment outside of a village that isn’t purchased by your group, crafted by your group, taken from an innocent human, or something that originally belonged to an ally, each PC gains experience points (XP) equal to the item’s total value in gc divided by the number of players. Unique items don’t have a cost. Their XP value is listed on their cards.

You keep track of the total XP your PC gains over the course of their adventuring career (called total XP or TXP), as well as XP you have spent.

You can only spend XP or gain bonuses from TXP when you finish a rest after earning the XP.

You can spend XP on new traits (see [[#Traits]]).

The Expertise & Stamina Advancement table shows the TXP required for your PC to increase their expertises or Stamina as well as the maximum number of uses an expertise can have. When you get a bonus, choose one of the following options:

- Gain three uses in expertises (including expertises you don’t already have) divided however you choose without exceeding the maximum number of uses for a single expertise.
- Increase your Stamina maximum by 2.
- Gain one expertise uses as described above, and increase your Stamina maximum by 1.

### Expertise & Stamina Advancement

| TXP | Bonus | Expertise Max Uses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 100 | 1st | 2 |
| 500 | 2nd | 2 |
| 1,250 | 3rd | 2 |
| 2,250 | 4th | 2 |
| 3,500 | 5th | 2 |
| 5,000 | 6th | 3 |
| 10,000 | 7th | 3 |
| 20,000 | 8th | 4 |
| 30,000 | 9th | 4 |
| Every 30,000 after | 10th and up | 4 |

The Characteristics Advancement table shows the TXP required for your PC to increase one of their characteristics by 1, to a maximum of 4. If all of your characteristics are 4 when you get this bonus, your Stamina maximum increases by 2 instead.

### Characteristics Advancement

| TXP | Bonus |
| --- | --- |
| 5,000 | 1st |
| 15,000 | 2nd |
| 30,000 | 3rd |
| Every 30,000 after | 4th and up |

## New PC After Death

If your PC dies and you make a new PC to take their place in the same campaign, you can roll on the Backgrounds table an additional number of times equal to the dead PC’s number of bonuses on the Expertise & Stamina Advancement table and choose any background for the new PC from the results you get.

> [!note] Starting With More
> If all of the other crows in the party have 5,000 or more TXP when you create a new PC, your character lags significantly behind the others in terms of power and survivability. This imbalance is a fun challenge for some players to offset with caution, equipment, and clever tactics. The disparity doesn’t last long. The new PC gains XP fast with experienced crows. For other groups, the new PC is a burden that leaves some folks feeling annoyed or ignored. To offset this unevenness, the Ref can decide that a new PC starts with XP equal to the lowest TXP of a crow already in the party.
> 
> It’s unlikely that a new crow will need better equipment than what they start with, since their dead counterpart likely left gear behind that they’ll get, but the Ref can give the PC gold pieces equal to half their starting XP to be used to buy better equipment. This money must be spent on equipment and not upgrading village institutions.

# Traits

Traits are character options that enhance your PC’s ability to create and use equipment. Your background grants you a trait. Traits are themed and grouped in trees.

## Buying Traits

Whenever you finish a rest, you can spend XP to buy one or more traits. You can only purchase a starting trait on a trait tree or a trait connected by a line to another trait you already have on the same tree. Starting traits appear at the top of trait trees and cost 500 XP each. You can only purchase a trait once.

## Minimum Modifier

Whenever a trait increases or decreases a number equal to one of your characteristics, the minimum number of that increase or decrease is 1, even if your characteristic is lower.

## Alchemy
*For creating and using acid, poisons, potions, and bombs*

### Midnight Oil
- **XP Cost:** 500 (Starting)
- When you work on crafting an alchemy item as part of the Craft Equipment rest activity, you can make two crafting rolls for the item or make a second crafting roll for a different alchemy item.

### It Burns
- **XP Cost:** 500 (Starting)
- Acids and poisons you craft deal additional damage equal to twice your Mind. Bombs you craft deal additional damage equal to your Mind.

### Potent Potion
- **XP Cost:** 500 (Starting)
- Healing potions you craft restore additional Stamina equal to your Mind or remove 1 additional wound if the drinker is already at full Stamina.

### Alchemy Epiphany
- **XP Cost:** 1,000 *(Requires Midnight Oil)*
- When you make a crafting roll for an alchemy item, you get a crit when the dice without modifiers equal 18, 19, or 20.

### Big Boom
- **XP Cost:** 1,000 *(Requires It Burns)*
- Bombs you craft have the size of the cube they affect increased by 1.

### Lasting Brew
- **XP Cost:** 1,000 *(Requires Potent Potion)*
- Potions and poisons you craft that have a duration listed in UD gain 1 additional UD.

### Eschew Ingredients
- **XP Cost:** 1,500 *(Requires Alchemy Epiphany)*
- You can ignore the material requirements for crafting an alchemy item if the item’s crafting goal is 100 or lower.

### You In Particular
- **XP Cost:** 1,500 *(Requires Big Boom)*
- When you use a bomb, choose one creature who must make the RR against its effects. That creature takes -2 penalty to the RR against the bomb.

### Potion to Gas
- **XP Cost:** 1,500 *(Requires Lasting Brew)*
- As an action, you can turn a potion you carry into a gas. The potion is immediately consumed and two creatures of your choice within 1 square of you, including you, are affected as if they drank the potion.

### Two for One
- **XP Cost:** 2,000 *(Requires Eschew Ingredients)*
- When you finish crafting an alchemy item, you craft two of the item instead of one.

### Don’t Waste a Drop
- **XP Cost:** 2,000 *(Requires You In Particular)*
- You can use an acid or a poison twice before it is consumed.

### Alchemy Belt
- **XP Cost:** 2,000 *(Requires Potion to Gas)*
- You gain an additional belt slot that can only be used to hold alchemy items.

## Alteration
*For maximal use of alteration spells*

### Lasting Alteration
- **XP Cost:** 500 (Starting)
- When you cast an alteration spell with a duration listed in UD, add an additional 1 UD to the duration.

### Grow Scales
- **XP Cost:** 500 (Starting)
- When you cast an alteration spell, you can harness the magic to grow temporary scales on your body and gain AD equal to your Mind. The AD granted by this trait disappears at the end of a rest.

### Alteration Stone
- **XP Cost:** 500 (Starting)
- As a rest activity, you can create an alteration stone (which has an equipment card). This stone disappears if you create a new one.

### Alteration Mastery
- **XP Cost:** 1,000 *(Requires Lasting Alteration)*
- Non-doom tier 1 results of rank 0 and 1 alteration spells you cast don’t add to the chaos count. Your alteration spells of rank 2 and higher are treated as 2 ranks lower when they trigger a backlash.

### Hooves
- **XP Cost:** 1,000 *(Requires Grow Scales)*
- When you cast an alteration spell, you can harness the magic to change your feet into powerful hooves until the end of the DT. While you have these hooves, your speed increases by your Mind.

### Morphing Stone
- **XP Cost:** 1,000 *(Requires Alteration Stone)*
- A creature who holds the stone can use a maneuver to change it into the shape of a mace, maul, flail, or back to its original form. The stone gains the stats of its weapon form while retaining its own stats.

### Improved Animal
- **XP Cost:** 1,500 *(Requires Alteration Mastery)*
- When you cast an alteration spell that allows you to turn into an animal, you can turn into an animal with power equal to what the spell says plus your Mind.

### Horns
- **XP Cost:** 1,500 *(Requires Hooves)*
- When you cast an alteration spell, you can harness the magic to grow horns from your head that last until the end of the DT. You can make an unarmed attack with the horns as a maneuver.

### Movement Stone
- **XP Cost:** 1,500 *(Requires Morphing Stone)*
- Add the following benefits to those your stone can confer:
  - You can breathe underwater and gain a swimming speed equal to your speed.
  - You gain a climbing speed equal to your speed.

### Split Alteration
- **XP Cost:** 2,000 *(Requires Improved Animal)*
- When you cast an alteration spell that has a target of 1 creature, object, ally, enemy, or target, you can target one additional qualifying target.

### Wings
- **XP Cost:** 2,000 *(Requires Horns)*
- When you cast an alteration spell, you can harness the magic to grow wings that last until the end of the DT. While you have these wings, you gain a flying speed equal to your speed.

### Spell Stone
- **XP Cost:** 2,000 *(Requires Movement Stone)*
- Choose up to three alteration spellbooks in your possession. The stone absorbs and destroys those books. A creature wielding the stone has those spellbooks equipped. The stone can only hold three spells.

## Archery
*For mastery of bows*

### Point Blank
- **XP Cost:** 500 (Starting)
- When you make a ranged attack with a bow, you don’t suffer the -1 penalty for having an enemy within 1 square of you.

### Range Finder
- **XP Cost:** 500 (Starting)
- While you wield a bow weapon, its range increases by 2 squares. This bonus stacks with Greater Range.

### Prone Position
- **XP Cost:** 500 (Starting)
- If you are prone, you gain a +1 bonus on attacks made with crossbows.

### Aim
- **XP Cost:** 1,000 *(Requires Point Blank)*
- You can use a maneuver to aim while you have a bow weapon equipped. If you make an attack with that weapon before the end of this turn, the attack gains a +1 bonus.

### Greater Range
- **XP Cost:** 1,000 *(Requires Range Finder)*
- While you wield a bow weapon, its range increases by 2 squares. This bonus stacks with Range Finder.

### Scrap Arrows
- **XP Cost:** 1,000 *(Requires Prone Position)*
- As a rest activity, you can turn sticks and scraps of nearby wood into arrows and gain 2d6 arrows when you finish the rest.

### Walls Can’t Save You
- **XP Cost:** 1,500 *(Requires Aim)*
- Attacks you make with bows don’t suffer a penalty for the target having cover.

### Shot in the Dark
- **XP Cost:** 1,500 *(Requires Greater Range)*
- Attacks you make with bows don’t suffer a penalty for the target being in dim light or similar concealment, and they only suffer a -2 penalty against targets in darkness or similar concealment.

### Double Tap
- **XP Cost:** 1,500 *(Requires Scrap Arrows)*
- When you make an attack with a bow, you can use two pieces of ammunition per target. If the attack hits, it deals additional damage equal to your Agility.

### Split Shot
- **XP Cost:** 2,000 *(Requires Walls Can’t Save You)*
- When you make an attack with a bow, you can use twice as much ammunition and select two targets within range for the attack.

### Careful Shot
- **XP Cost:** 2,000 *(Requires Shot in the Dark)*
- When you get a non-doom tier 1 result on an attack made with a bow, you don’t hit an ally within 1 square of the target.

# Equipment

An adventurer’s equipment is essential to their survival. Torches, swords, spellbooks, rations, and more make the difference between a big payday and your last day ever.

## Inventory Cards

Items that you can hold in your inventory are detailed on cards that are included with this game. These cards contain the item’s game statistics as shown on this page.

### Marking Cards

There isn’t a card included for every variation of an item within the game. For example, alchemist’s tools and masterwork alchemist’s tool use the same card, as do an iron sword and a steel sword. If you’re using a digital version of the game, you can copy and modify cards or print them out and write on them as you like.

If you have a physical copy of this game *(Playtest note: no one does yet)*, clear plastic card sleeves were included with it. An inventory card fits inside of a sleeve and you can write on the sleeve with dry erase marker. All physical versions include the digital files, so you can also print out more cards as needed.

### Blank Cards

Blank cards are included with the game to allow the Ref to create their own equipment as needed.

> [!tip] Think Outside the Card
> Inventory cards detail specific ways you can use an item, but that’s not all you can do with that equipment. You can do anything with it that you could do with in real life. Just because a sword’s card doesn’t say anything about it being used as an impromptu pry bar, doesn’t mean you can’t use it that way. You can turn some 11-foot poles and a tent into a raft! The sword might break, and the raft might sink, but isn’t all that gold worth giving it a shot?

## Money

The currency of Cornath is gold coins (gc). An inventory slot can hold 250 loose coins or 1 purse that holds up to 500 gc.

## Shopping

The equipment the PCs can buy depends on the level of the merchants in their village. See [[#The Village]] for more information.

## Armor

You can only benefit from a shield’s AD by wielding it. You can only benefit from a suit of armor’s AD by wearing it. To wear a suit of armor, it must be in your backpack slots. You can only wear one suit of armor at a time. You can’t don or doff a suit of armor when you are not in a situation tracked in combat rounds.

### Armor Types

There are four types of armor, each with its own inventory card:

- **Shields:** Metal shields of all shapes
- **Light:** Cloth, hide, and leather
- **Medium:** Chain and scale mail
- **Heavy:** Plate, ring, and splint mail

## Armor Enchantments

Armor enchantments can be added to suits of armor or shields. The Armor Enchantments table shows the number of Enchanting uses, materials, and crafting goal required to enchant the armor and if the enchantment can be placed on a shield, suit of armor, or both. It also shows the price for buying the enchantment. If you buy armor with an enchantment on it, add this price to the armor’s base price.

A suit of armor or shield can contain multiple enchantments. The total number of expertise uses needed to craft all of an armor’s enchantments can’t exceed 4.

Some armor has unique enchantments that aren’t listed here. The information for crafting that armor is listed on the item’s card.

- **Banishing:** When a creature hits you with a melee attack while you wear or wield this armor, you can use a reaction to push them 1d6 squares. If a creature gets a crit on a melee attack against you while you wear or wield this armor, you fly vertically up 1d6 × 10 feet and then fall and can’t use your reaction to activate this armor in response to the attack.
- **Climbing:** While you wear this armor, you gain a climbing speed equal to your walking speed. If you are knocked prone while climbing, you immediately fall and this armor’s enchantment ceases to function until the end of the DT.
- **Dancing:** As a maneuver while wielding this shield, you command it to float in the air and follow and protect you, freeing it from your hand slot though you are still considered wielding it, until the end of the DT. A creature who scores a crit against you while the shield is floating can grab it and wield it (no action required), causing the shield to stop floating. The shield can’t be made to float again until you finish a rest. You can only have one dancing shield active at a time.
- **Deep:** While wearing this armor, you gain a swimming speed equal to your walking speed and can’t suffocate. When you fall while wearing this armor, the fall damage increases by 1d6.
- **Demon’s Head:** A demon’s head is emblazoned on both sides of this shield. While you wield it, you deal additional damage with a counter equal to your Agility or Strength (your choice) as the outer face bites your target. When you take damage from a counter, you take an additional 1 damage as the inner face bites you.
- **Feather:** While you wear this armor, whenever you fall, the damage is reduced by 3d6. When you are force moved, you can be moved 1 additional square.
- **Flying:** As a maneuver while wielding this shield, you can place it on the ground and make it hover. While hovering, a Medium or smaller creature can ride on the shield and gains a flying speed equal to their walking speed and counts as wielding the shield. The shield can fly until the end of the DT or until it loses all of its AD, at which point it and any creature using it to fly falls. Any attacks or damaging effects made against the creature flying that originate from below first damage the shield before any other armor the creature wears. The shield can’t be made to fly again until you finish a rest.
- **Glow:** While you wear or wield this armor, you can use a maneuver to make it glow, giving off light 5/5. This effect has 2 UD and the UD recharge when you finish a rest. Once you turn this effect on, it can’t be turned off until the UD expire. If you roll a doom while wearing, wielding, or carrying the armor and the light effect is available but hasn’t been turned off, the effect activates.
- **Heavy:** While you wear this armor and are force moved, you reduce the move by 1. If you are knocked prone, you must use your action to stand up instead of a maneuver.
- **Luring:** When another creature hits you with a melee attack while you wear or wield this armor, you can use a reaction to slide them 1 square. If a creature gets a crit against you with an attack, you can’t use this enchantment’s reaction until the end of the DT and the creature can slide you 1 square.
- **Passthrough:** While you wear this armor, you use a maneuver to move up to your speed. While you move, mundane walls and other obstructions can’t hamper your movement. If you end this move inside of a solid object, you are shunted back to the space where you entered it and take 1d6 P damage for each space you are moved through on your way back. This maneuver has a 1 UD that must be rolled after activating the effect. The UD recharge when you finish a rest.
- **Revenge:** When you take damage while wearing this armor it gains 1d6 charges. When you hit a creature with a melee attack, you can expend all charges built up in the armor and deal additional damage equal to the charges spent. If the charges reach 12 or higher, they are all expended and you take P damage equal to the charges spent. All charges in the armor disappear at the end of a DT.
- **Silent:** While wearing this armor, you gain a +1 bonus to tests made to hide and sneak. If you get a tier 1 result on a test made to hide or sneak, you are weakened.
- **Slick:** This armor secretes a non-flammable oil. While wearing it, you can move 1 additional square when you use the Shift maneuver and you gain a +1 bonus to the Escape Grab maneuver’s test. When you roll a doom on an Agility or Strength test, you fall prone.
- **Speedy:** While you wear this armor, your speed increases by 1. If an attack against you is a crit, your speed is reduced to 0 until the end of your next combat turn.
- **Spell-Storing:** As a rest activity or as part of the Repair Armor rest activity while repairing this armor, you can touch a spellbook to it. While you wear or wield the armor, you can cast the spell as if you were holding the spellbook. If you roll a doom on any test while wearing, wielding, or carrying the armor, the spell is immediately cast, and the Ref picks the targets. If there are no viable targets, add 1d6 to the chaos count and the spell is expended from the armor. Once you cast the spell, it is gone from the armor. The armor can only store one spell at a time. When a spell is placed in it, any old spells are gone from the armor.
- **Sustaining:** While you wear this armor, you don’t need to eat to avoid starvation or gain the benefits of a rest. If you die while wearing this armor, it consumes any items you have in your hand and belt slots when you die.
- **Telepathic Node:** As a rest activity while wearing this armor, you can choose up to five other willing humans who rest with you to become connected to the armor until you use this rest activity again or die. While you wear this armor, any of the creatures connected to the armor can communicate telepathically with each other and you as long as they have line of effect to you and you can communicate telepathically with them regardless of distance or obstacles. If a creature connected to the armor dies, you and the other creatures connected take 1d10 P damage. If you die, connected creatures take 2d10 P damage.
- **Victory:** When you kill another creature while wearing this armor, you are blessed. If you have a wound while wearing this armor, you can’t be blessed by any effect.
- **Waterwalking:** While wearing this armor, you can walk on the surface of any liquid as if it were solid ground. If you fall prone while standing on liquid, this armor’s enchantment ceases to function until the end of the DT and you take a -2 penalty on tests made to swim.

## Weapons

A weapon’s equipment card shows how much damage it deals on a tier 2 or 3 result with an attack. It doesn’t show the tier 1 result, which is always a miss.

### Weapon Types

A weapon’s type determines the expertise that can be applied to attacks made with it. A weapon can be a bashing, bow, chopping, slashing, stabbing, or unarmed weapon.

### Weapon Qualities

A weapon can have any of the following qualities:

- **Brutal:** When you get a crit on an attack with this weapon, you deal twice as much damage with the attack as you normally would.
- **Cumbersome:** The weapon only occupies 1 slot on your backpack or belt but requires 2 hand slots to wield.
- **Disengage:** When you take the Shift maneuver while wielding this weapon, you can move 1 additional square as part of the maneuver. If you wield two weapons with this quality, the benefits stack.
- **Dismember:** When you score a crit on an attack made with this weapon, it removes one of the target’s limbs. Roll on the Dismember table to determine the limb removed.
- **Light:** When you hit with a melee attack while wielding two light weapons, you deal additional damage equal to the unused weapon’s tier 2 result (without the inclusion of your Agility or Strength as part of the damage). While a hand slot is empty, it counts as a light weapon that can be used to make unarmed strikes.
- **Parry X:** While you wield this weapon, you can use it to absorb damage as if it were a shield. The weapon has AD equivalent to X. When the weapon’s AD is 0, it takes a -1 damage penalty. You can repair the weapon as if it were a shield using the Repair Armor rest activity.
- **Pummeling:** When you get a tier 3 result with an attack made with this weapon against a creature who is your size or smaller, you can push them 1 square. When you get a crit on an attack with this weapon, you can knock a target of your size or smaller prone.
- **Reload:** You must use a maneuver to load 1 piece of ammunition into this weapon before it can be used to attack. After being used to attack, it must be loaded again before it can be used to make another attack.

## Pets

Pets are animals who follow a single human owner. You own pets you buy. You can give a pet you own to another willing human who becomes their owner. If you meet a friendly animal with no owner, you can become their owner with the following test:

**2d10 + M**
- **≤11 (Tier 1):** The animal refuses to engage with you.
- **12–16 (Tier 2):** The animal follows you at a distance for the next 24 hours, but doesn’t heed your commands. If you spend your next rest activity bonding with them, you become the owner when you finish the rest.
- **17+ (Tier 3):** You become the animal’s owner.

### Pet Stats

A pet has backpack inventory slots that can be used to carry equipment for their owner. A pet comes outfitted with a special harness that holds equipment and if the animal is Large or larger, a saddle and other equipment required to ride it. These backpack inventory slots take wounds just like a PC’s.

You can ride any pet who is larger than you. You occupy 6 of a creature’s inventory slots when you ride them.

Pets need to eat each day just like PCs and must eat during a rest to get the benefits of a rest. While traveling in the wild, a pet can hunt and forage for themselves, but in a dungeon or other place with no food sources, they must consume animal feed (the equivalent of rations for them). A Large pet needs twice the amount of animal feed to gain the benefits of a rest and avoid starvation, a Huge pet needs four times the amount, and a Holy Shit pet needs eight times the amount. Pets are not equipment.

## Vehicles

Carts, wagons, and the like require animals to move across the land. These vehicles can be loaded up with treasure and supplies to make excursions to distant dungeons more survivable and worth the trek.

The Vehicles table shows each vehicle’s price, Stamina, size, slots for how much equipment it can hold, and the number of Large animals required to move it. A Huge creature counts as two Large animals for the purposes of moving a vehicle and a Holy Shit! creature counts as eight. Animals hauling a land vehicle can’t serve as mounts or have any of their slots occupied by equipment. All of their slots are considered filled by the vehicle they pull. A vehicle’s speed and pace is that of the slowest animal moving it.

## Hirelings

Hirelings are NPCs you can hire to come with you on an adventure. Their stat blocks and equipment can be found in the *Bestiary*.

### Employment Terms

Each hireling has these employment terms, which are standard in Cornath:

- Each hireling expects payment and a day’s worth of food at the start of each day they spend in the service of a PC. The daily gc payment for a hireling is equal to their power multiplied by 10 (minimum 10 gc).
- If a hireling dies while in the service of a PC, all of their equipment (or treasure of equivalent value) and salary already paid plus a death payment equal to the hireling’s power multiplied by 500 gc is owed to their family upon returning to the village where the hireling was hired.
- If a hireling doesn’t receive payment, they leave the service of the PC. If they don’t receive their daily payment or if they die and their family doesn’t receive their equipment, payment, and death benefit, word gets around that the PC and other crows associated with them are jerks, and other hirelings refuse to work with them until the debt is paid.

### Controlling Hirelings

Hirelings know the danger of working with crows. For the most part, the player of the PC who hired the hireling controls the hireling for their employment period. The Ref reserves the right to take control of the hireling if the player has them do something very out of character or outlandishly dangerous for little to no reward.

Hirelings follow the same rules as PCs, except that they can’t gain or spend XP. They can use equipment, have expertises, and take all the same actions, maneuvers, and rest activities. They are willing to use equipment given to them by the PCs and willing to return anything borrowed.

### Death of a PC

If a hireling currently in the employment of a PC learns of that PC’s death, they approach any living PCs at the earliest convenience and ask if those PCs would like to continue working with them. If there are no PCs willing to work with the hireling, the hireling returns to the village where they were hired.

# The Village

Crows die and retire, but their village remains. With Cornath recovering from near destruction, your village is one of the only places left for humans to survive. Invest in your village, and your community will thrive and better prepare you for delving into ruins. If you ignore your village, it will decay, leaving you without a place to buy equipment, sell treasure, or safely survive your time outside of dungeons.

In Cornath every village is contained within a ruin. These structures are enclosed, protecting the people within from the Miasma and the monsters wandering the wilds.

> [!note] Other Villages
> As you travel from one dungeon to another, you might stop in a village other than your own to rest or trade. These villages function the same way as your own, except you can’t invest in them and the Ref doesn’t have to worry about tracking their prosperity or events. The Ref determines the level of each institution in another village.
> 
> If you want to invest in more than one village, ask the Ref. You’ll need to start tracking Prosperity, events, and all the other stats for that village yourself. It’s a challenge to keep up with more than one village. Don’t attempt something like this until you have the hang of it with one.

## Starting Village

At the start of a campaign, your village institutions include a blacksmith, crypt, general store, inn, and temple. As a group, choose one other institution already established in your village. All these institutions are 1st level. Then come up with a name for your village as a group.

## NPC Connection

When you create a village or create a new PC that’s part of an existing village, you should create an NPC who lives in that village who is important to your PC in some way. They’re likely a relative, friend, mentor, or someone else who matters a lot to you. This NPC is called a connection since they help link your character to the village. Give this NPC a name, define their relationship to you, and pick one of the following benefits that your connection gives you:

- **Animal Lover:** The connection loves animals, and will look after any of your pets while they are in the village, feeding them for free. Animals that rest with this connection heal 2 additional wounds, or 3 additional wounds if the village’s Prosperity is 6 or higher.
- **Caretaker:** The connection likes to look out for the people they care about. When you rest in the village, you can rest in their home. If you do, you heal 2 additional wounds, or 3 additional wounds if the village’s Prosperity is 6 or higher.
- **Concerned:** This connection is worried about you whenever you leave the village. Once per cycle when you leave the village, they give you 1 torch.
- **Crafty:** The connection has a natural ability to aid in crafting items, though they lack formal training. When you make a crafting roll in the village, you gain a +2 bonus to the roll.
- **Foodie:** This connection loves to make food for their friends. Once per cycle when you leave the village, they provide you with a number of rations equal to half the village’s Prosperity (minimum 1 ration).
- **Magic Enthusiast:** This connection has gathered a lot of knowledge and texts about magic items. Each day, they can examine a number of magic items equal to the village’s Prosperity (minimum 1 magic item) and learn all of its properties and share them with you.
- **Money Bags:** The connection is willing to lend you money. While you’re in the village, you can ask them for a loan of up to 100 × the village’s Prosperity gc (minimum 100 gc). The connection won’t grant you another loan until any previous loan is paid back in full.
- **Monster Collector:** The connection enjoys collecting and trading monster parts. They are willing to trade monster parts you have for ones that they need. They trade 1 monster part of a specific monster type for 5 monster parts of another type.
- **Rival:** This connection is a rival crow. Once per cycle when you leave the village and go to a dungeon, they make a bet with you to see who will have a more monetarily successful adventure away from town. When you return, roll any die. On an even result, you win. On an odd result, they win. The loser pays the winner 50 gc.
- **Smarty Pants:** This connection finds research exciting. While you are in the village, you can ask this connection a question, and the Ref will tell you if the answer is something the NPC can find out through researching in books or by talking to experts in the village. If they can, the connection provides the answer 1 day later. The connection can only research 1 question at a time.

## Village Cycle

Village cycles track when events occur, when new institutions get established and upgraded, and when merchants restock. A village cycle is 10 days long.

## Prosperity

Your village’s Prosperity is a numerical representation of how happy and economically well off the people of your village are. As institutions improve, so do opportunities for the villagers. The higher the Prosperity, the more villagers care about each other, and seek to protect and help their neighbors. If Prosperity falls, the village’s infrastructure begins to degrade. With less money coming into the town, there are fewer opportunities for villagers, and many become destitute or turn to crime to live.

At the start of a campaign, your village’s Prosperity is 0, which is average. It’s not doing amazing, but it’s not doing terrible. This number is important in determining item availability (see [[#Trade]]) and the type of events your village experiences (see [[#Village Events]]).

### Raising Prosperity

When you invest the money to found or upgrade an institution, you increase your village’s Prosperity by 1. If you spend at least 10,000 gc in a village during a cycle on goods from merchant institutions, the village’s prosperity increases by 1. The highest Prosperity a village can have is 10.

### Lowering Prosperity

When a village ends a cycle during which nothing occurred that could raise its Prosperity, its Prosperity decreases by 1. The lowest Prosperity a village can have is -10.

## Village Events

At the end of each village cycle, the Ref rolls for an event that occurs during the next village cycle (which happens whether or not the PCs are in the village). This event impacts the village or the PCs in some way. The higher the village’s Prosperity, the more likely the event is to be a boon to the PCs. The lower the Prosperity, the more likely the event is to have a negative impact.

When a village event occurs, roll on the Village Event table to determine what happens. Unless otherwise specified, when an institution, item, or PC is referenced by the table, that institution, item, or PC is randomly chosen by the Ref.

*(Playtest Note: The Village Event table will have more events, thus decreasing the odds for any one event.)*

You can buy or trade goods in your village, provided you have merchants who can supply them. A merchant’s description in [[#Institutions]] lists what they buy and sell.

### Buying and Availability

Unless otherwise noted, items are purchased for the price listed in the rules, either in a booklet or on their cards.

## Village Crafting

Some institutions, called artisans, provide crafting services for PCs. If the PC can’t find a craftable item they want and wish to craft it themselves, they can pay the institution with a high enough crafting bonus to do it.

- To begin the project, the institution must be given the crafting materials for the item and payment, which is the item’s full price.
- The institution makes one crafting roll per day toward crafting the item (because they have more than just your business on their plate). The institution’s stats include the bonus you should add to this crafting roll. You should keep track of the crafting points.
- If you pay twice as much, the institution makes it a rush job and makes two crafting rolls a day for the item.
- When the item is complete, you can visit the institution and pick it up.

# Institutions

Institutions are the merchants, temples, and other sites, services, and organizations that you can interact with while you are in the town.

You should write down and keep track of each institution’s level and its steward, which is the person who owns or is otherwise in charge of it. Stewards are NPCs played by the Ref. When you found an institution in your town, the Ref can provide the name and personality of the steward, or they can ask the player whose character invests in the institution to create the steward.

Each institution has the following details:

- **Founding Price:** The price to establish the institution in your village. When you pay to establish an institution, it doesn’t start operating until the start of the next village cycle.
- **Services:** The services provided by the institution are detailed here. Some services are only available when an institution achieves a specific level.
- **Level:** Each institution has a level that increases when you pay to advance it. An institution’s starting level is 1. Its maximum level is detailed on its advancement table.
- **Advancement Table:** An institution’s advancement table shows the price to increase an institution’s level, and the benefits that come from advancing the institution. When you pay to upgrade an institution, it doesn’t start operating at its new level until the start of the next village cycle. Merchant item availability is shown on this table.

- **Boon of Rescue:** After you roll an RR, you can use this boon to improve the result by 1 tier. You can use this boon a number of times equal to the crypt’s level before it is expended.
- **Boon of Swiftness:** When you expend this boon, your speed increases by a number equal to the crypt’s level until the end of the DT.
- **Boon of Vitality:** When you regain Stamina, you can expend this boon to regain additional Stamina equal to twice the crypt’s level.

You have a home in your village, so you don’t need to stay the night at the inn. However, if you’re in another town (or want to stay at the inn in your village for some reason), the cost of a night at the inn, which includes a day’s worth of food, is 5 gc.

#### Gambling
As a rest activity, you can gamble at the common room of the inn. The maximum bet you can place is based on the inn’s level. Minimum bet is 1 gc. You can play the following games at the inn:
- **Over/Under 7:** State whether you think the result of a 2d6 roll will be over, under, or exactly 7 and make a bet. Then roll the dice. If you were wrong, you lose your bet. If you were right and guessed over or under 7, your money is doubled. If you guessed a result of exactly 7 right, your money is quadrupled.
- **Guess the Number:** You must guess a number from 1 to 6. Then the Ref rolls a d6. If you guessed the result incorrectly, you lose your bet. If you guessed correctly, your winnings are five times your bet.

#### Find It Here
When the inn is 5th level and the village’s Prosperity is 10, you can find a special traveling merchant at the inn. Once per cycle, you can purchase any item that has a price from this merchant, but the cost of the item is increased by 25%.

The stables have the following services:

- **Animal Feed:** They buy and sell animal feed.

#### Artisan
The priests of the Smith can craft alchemy, blacksmithing, and enchanting items and enchant weapons and armor with any enchantments that they are given the materials for. They make crafting rolls with a bonus equal to the temple’s level.

#### Merchant

## Your Home

Every player character has a small living space they call home within their village. This house is a secure place to keep equipment and treasure that you keep stocked with food. While you’re in your village, you don’t need to worry about tracking money to spend on food and shelter.

## Retirement

When your PC has 60,000 or more TXP, you can retire them to the village. If you do, your PC becomes an NPC and no longer delves into dungeons. You can give your equipment to a worthy successor, perhaps a new PC you create or one of the other crows your retired adventurer delved alongside. A retired PC gives up the dangerous life of dungeon delving.

When you retire to the town, choose one of the following benefits that the town gains. If you have 100,000 or more TXP, you can choose two benefits:

*(Playtest Note: More retirement benefits will be in a future version of the test.)*

- **Best Steward Ever:** Your retired PC becomes the steward of an institution in the village (taking over from the previous steward). This institution always operates as if the village’s Prosperity is 10, even if it is lower.
- **Crow Daddy:** Your retired PC is sure to give helpful tips to new crows. Choose one starting trait your retired PC knows. When you or another player creates a new PC from the village, that PC can start with that trait in addition to any other traits they start with. A new PC with 0 TXP can start with a maximum of three traits.
- **Generous Benefactor:** Your retired NPC still loves to hear stories about the crow life and hangs out at the inn common room or a tavern listening to all the latest adventures. Once per cycle, the PCs can visit your retired PC who gives them a gift rolled on the Minor Things table in *The Ref*.
- **Master Mentor:** Choose up to three traits your retired PC knows. Other PCs can learn these traits from your retired PC, making them cost half as much XP as they normally do.
- **Work With My Hands:** Your retired PC goes to work at an artisan institution in the village. This institution gains an additional +4 bonus to crafting rolls, and provides an additional +4 bonus to anyone renting and working in the institution’s workshop, if it has one.

## Not Your Village

When you travel to a village that isn’t your home, the Ref determines the village’s Prosperity and available institutions. The Ref doesn’t need to track cycles or events for these villages. They can decide what is there based on whatever makes the most sense for the story. More advice on making these villages is in *The Ref*. *(Playtest note: No it’s not ... yet.)*

## Founding Other Villages

There may come a time when you and your fellow players wish to found another village. Perhaps you want the challenge of starting fresh, or maybe you feel you’ve raided all the nearby dungeons and want a new home closer to fresh loot!

You can create a new village in a suitable ruin by spending 15,000 gc. It takes ten days for your new village to be established and for the starting institutions to be founded. This new village can have NPCs, including your connections and retired crows, relocate to it as you wish.

When you create a new village, that one becomes your home. You can visit your old village still and benefit from its institutions, but the village’s Prosperity is now tracked by the Ref.
