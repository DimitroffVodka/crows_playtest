# 04 Crows Dungeons Book for Playtest 2

> Source PDF: `04 Crows Dungeons Book for Playtest 2.pdf`
> Page count: 19

Prepare to crawl, dungeon style! This book, meant for the Ref’s eyes only, presents many POIs, villages, and dungeons in Cornath, but you can put them in any world. You can use the Cornath map to judge travel distances and the Overland Travel section in *The Rules* to determine how long each journey takes and what happens during them. The statistics for creatures in these dungeons can be found in *The Ref*.

---

# Village: Gadwick

Gadwick is contained in the towers and expansive dungeon and crypts of the once-grand Castle Gadwick. It has a Prosperity of 0 and the following institutions and their stewards:

- **1st-Level Alchemist:** Brune
- **1st-Level Auction House:** Lili
- **2nd-Level Barracks:** Cormal
- **2nd-Level Blacksmith:** Deirdre
- **1st-Level Bookseller:** Rion
- **1st-Level Enchanter:** Isaac
- **3rd-Level General Store:** Sorcha
- **3rd-Level Stables:** Anna
- **1st-Level Temple:** Mackle
- **1st-Level Inn:** Duna
- **1st-Level Crypt:** Oda. *Boons available:* Greed (1), Rescue (1), Vitality (1).

---

> [!tip] Awarding Treasures
> If the PCs find a magic item they’ve never encountered before, they aren’t aware of the item’s properties (though they can tell the item is magic based on how it vibrates when touched). You can give the players a generic card when they find a magic item instead of the specific item’s card to keep properties hidden. If the players find a *boom wand*, give them the magic wand card.

> [!note] Guide Not Handcuffs
> The text here presents challenges, but can’t account for every way to overcome them. Don’t let that get in the way of fun. If the PCs have a clever idea that isn’t accounted for in the text, let them try it! If it makes sense and should just work, it does. If it seems risky, call for a test.
> 
> *(Playtest Note: This book will have more POIs, villages, and dungeons in future versions.)*

# Dungeon Hooks

The PCs might discover a dungeon in one of the following ways. You can choose a story hook from the following list or create your own:

- Give the players a map of the region with known dungeons, villages, and POIs on it, and allow them to gather rumors on their own and decide which they feel is best suited to the PCs. This isn’t just a map the players have, it’s also one that their characters have, given to them by an NPC connection or found on a corpse.
- The PCs get word from an NPC connection or friendly NPC crow about a dungeon they overheard being discussed at the local tavern.
- The PCs learn of the route to a dungeon from a merchant passing through their village.
- The PCs find a map to a dungeon left behind by a group of NPC crows who went out to the location and haven’t returned.

# POI: Ruined Tower

When the PCs reach the tower, they find:
- A tower, completely collapsed into a pile of stone rubble and rotting timbers.
- A ring of stones around a hole in the ground that used to be a well.

## Collapsed Tower

A PC who investigates the rubble finds some of the stones are marked with arcane runes, a sign that this place once belonged to a mage. One or more PCs can search through the densely-packed rubble, but doing so earns them an encounter check with an EN of 7. If an encounter occurs, use the Travel Encounters table in *The Ref*.

If the PCs wish to search faster, they can use brute force. If they go this route, each PC searching makes a Strength test:

**2d10 + S**
- **≤11 (Tier 1):** Take 1d6 + 3 P dam.
- **12–16 (Tier 2):** Take 1d6 P dam.
- **17+ (Tier 3):** No effect.

A PC who searches the rubble finds something on the Minor Interesting Things table in *The Ref*. The tower can produce three rolls on the table and then no more.

## Well

The well descends into darkness and has smooth sides leading down 40 feet to a dusty dry bottom where a human skeleton in tattered rags sits slumped on the ground with a shovel in hand.

A PC who climbs down into the well notices the skeleton’s left leg is broken. The remains belong to a crow who broke their leg climbing into the well and buried treasure in the hopes of returning for it if they were rescued. They weren’t.

If a PC digs into the earth of the well, they find a **coin purse** that contains **94 gc**.

# POI: Ruined Windmill

When the PCs reach the windmill, they find:
- A crumbling stone windmill, tattered sails creaking as they spin in the breeze.
- The upper walls of the 40-foot-tall windmill sport large holes.
- A rotting wood door hangs off its hinges, leading the way into the first floor.

## Mill First Floor

- The remnants of destroyed boxes and torn burlap litter the floor.
- The ceiling above creaks and rattles as the windmill’s sails turn.
- A ladder leads up 10 feet to the second floor.

### Floor Rubble
The rubble on the floor makes the ground on this level difficult terrain.

A PC who searches through the rubble on the floor finds a door that leads into a cellar. The wood door is swollen from moisture and stuck shut, but can be forced open with a crowbar or cut with a tool; however, doing so wakes the unconscious **undead C** in the cellar.

If a creature wants to attempt a gentler opening of the door, they can make the following test:

**2d10 + S**
- **≤11 (Tier 1):** The PC opens the door and falls into the cellar.
- **12–16 (Tier 2):** The PC opens the door quietly, but drops an item from their belt into the cellar.
- **17+ (Tier 3):** The PC opens the door quietly.

It is 4 squares from the first floor to the cellar. The ladder that connected the floors broke.

## Cellar

The cellar is shrouded in darkness.
- *(If the undead C didn’t leave this area to attack)* A large undead creature lies on the ground.
- Human bones gnawed clean litter the dirt floor.

The unconscious **undead C** here wakes if disturbed, attempting to grab a PC and take them away to consume them.

### Among the Bones
Searching through the bones finds **54 gc** and an item from the Major Interesting Things table.

## Second Floor

- The floors above the second have collapsed into it, leaving a pile of debris on the groaning floor, including heavy stones for grinding wheat.
- A spinning wood shaft hangs down from the top of the ceiling, where something glitters.

### Collapsing Floor
When a creature steps onto the floor, it gives a loud creak under the added weight. If two or more Medium or larger creatures add their weight to the floor, the second level collapses down into the first, and each creature on the second and first level must make the following Agility RR. Creatures on the first level take a bane on this test. If the floor collapses, make an encounter check with an EN of 8. If an encounter occurs, use the Travel Encounters table in *The Ref*.

**2d10 + A**
- **≤11 (Tier 1):** 10 dam
- **12–16 (Tier 2):** 5 dam
- **17+ (Tier 3):** No effect

### The Shaft
The spinning shaft is connected to the sails outside. As long as they spin, the shaft spins. A **steel knife** is stuck into the top of the shaft. If a PC attempts to climb the shaft while it is spinning, they must make the following test:

**2d10 + S**
- **≤11 (Tier 1):** The PC reaches the top of the shaft but falls off before they can grab the knife.
- **12–16 (Tier 2):** The PC reaches the top of the shaft and can grab the knife, but then they fall.
- **17+ (Tier 3):** The PC can climb up, grab the knife, then climb back safely.

The shaft leads up to the top of the ceiling, which is 6 squares above the second floor and 8 squares above the first floor. If the PC got a tier 1 result on the test to climb the shaft and falls onto the floor on the second level, the floor collapses.

# Dungeon: Blood Library

Before Craelin the Clever became Craelin the Craven, he built a repository of books to share his knowledge of alteration magic with the world. As he fell into wickedness, the necromancer covered this library in flesh and filled it with blood creatures to protect what he once shared. With Craelin gone, ambitious crows have hacked through the protective scab to form an entrance into the Blood Library, where books and other treasures are ripe for picking.

## Library Arrival

When the PCs reach the Blood Library, they notice the following details:
- An enormous hunk of scabby flesh covered in giant, moving eyes weeps blood and pus into a red lake.
- The lake flows into a tall, wide wound in the scab that leads to stone steps that rise up out of the red.
- A pyramid-shaped, dark blue glass skylight peeks out the top of the scab.

The blood lake and library entrance are depicted on the Blood Library map.

### Blood Lake

A PC can make the following test to see what they know about the blood lake:

**2d10 + M**
- **≤11 (Tier 1):** No info.
- **12–16 (Tier 2):** Blood creatures are attracted to blood.
- **17+ (Tier 3):** A creature covered in blood can’t hide from a blood creature.

The blood lake is 3 feet deep and easy to wade through, though a PC who enters the lake has their clothes stained with blood. A PC who is covered in blood takes a bane on tests made to sneak and hide from blood creatures. A PC can clean the blood from their clothes as a rest activity.

The PCs may be able to avoid wading through the blood by climbing the fleshy mound that surrounds the library. No test is required if they use sharp weapons or tools to stab into it to hold their balance. They may also cut down trees to build a bridge to cross.

## Entering the Library

The PCs can enter the library by either going through the entrance (area 1), or by climbing up the scab and smashing through the skylight, which leads them to area 3. The skylight is painted an opaque dark blue to filter out light and doesn’t open.

> [!important] First Dungeon Turn
> **When the PCs enter the library, the first Dungeon Turn begins.** Let them know, and start the 30-minute timer.

### Shared Details

Unless otherwise noted, interior areas of the library share the following details:
- **Darkness:** The interior areas of the library have no light sources.
- **Flesh-Covered Walls:** The walls of the library are covered in scabby flesh and can be climbed without a test by PCs who stab into the walls with sharp weapons or tools.
- **Floor Heights:** The second floor of the library is 15 feet above the first. The ceiling of the second floor is 15 feet high.
- **Map:** Areas of the library are keyed to the Blood Library map.
- **Noticeable Details:** Every area starts with a bulleted list of details the PCs notice when they enter for the first time, provided they can discern the details in the darkness.

## Library Encounters

The EN for the library is 9. If at least one PC is bloodstained by the lake, the EN is 8. If half or more of the PCs are bloodstained, the EN is 7. When a random encounter occurs, roll on the Blood Creature Encounters table in *The Ref*.

This area is filled with dim light thanks to the open entrance leading outdoors.
- A massive stone desk stands before three sets of doors.
- A pearl-handled letter opener is wedged through the handles of a south-facing cabinet double door at the bottom of the desk.

### Cabinet
The letter opener is a **Tiny art object worth 20 gc**. If it is removed from the cabinet door, the **blood creature B** trapped inside of it bursts forth, eager to devour the closest PC.

## 2. Library Ground

- Mostly empty shelves hold a few ruined books.
- Spiral staircases in each corner lead up to a balcony that lines the walls above.
- *(If the blood creatures are still in area 3)* The sound of several mouths gnawing on something wet and juicy comes from above.

### Shelves
The shelves here are mostly empty save for a **lore book (Magic Lore)** that can be found if a PC spends 5 minutes searching the shelves.

### Blood Creatures
The blood creatures in area 3 come to investigate if the PCs make any loud noises or ignite a light source that gives off bright light of 10 squares or more.

---

## 3. Library Balcony

- *(If the blood creatures haven’t gone to area 2)* Several blood creatures—six-armed, short humanoids with razor teeth—cling to the walls here, feasting on the flesh.
- Mostly empty shelves line the back wall of a balcony that overlooks more library below.
- A closed cabinet stands on the west wall, just south of a door, painted with a red droplet.

### Blood Creatures
There are two **blood creature As** per member of the PC’s party. The creatures fight until the PCs outnumber them, then flee. If the PCs flee this area, the creatures don’t pursue, content to continue munching on the walls.

### Shelves
The shelves here are mostly empty save for two **lore books (Historical Lore and Nature Lore)** that can be found if a PC spends 5 minutes searching the shelves.

### Locked Cabinet
The cabinet is locked and trapped. A PC who examines the keyhole notices a small arcane rune carved above it and can decipher its meaning with the following test:

**2d10 + M**
- **≤11 (Tier 1):** No info.
- **12–16 (Tier 2):** The rune means “fire.”
- **17+ (Tier 3):** The rune is a magic trap. If the lock is tampered with, the cabinet explodes.

If the key in area 6 is used to unlock the cabinet, the trap is disarmed. A PC can attempt to pick its lock, taking a bane on the test if they use a tool other than a lockpick set:

**2d10 + A**
- **≤11 (Tier 1):** The trap triggers.
- **12–16 (Tier 2):** The cabinet is opened with enough time for the opener to grab one item from it before the trap triggers.
- **17+ (Tier 3):** The cabinet unlocks and the trap is disarmed.

If a creature attempts to force the cabinet open with brute Strength or damages the door or lock, the trap triggers. When the trap triggers, the cabinet explodes and everything inside of it is destroyed. Each creature within 2 squares of it must make an Agility RR:

**2d10 + A**
- **≤11 (Tier 1):** 2d6 dam
- **12–16 (Tier 2):** 1d6 dam
- **17+ (Tier 3):** 0 dam

The cabinet contains a ***healing potion***, a ***shrink* spellbook**, and a **poison vial**.

## 4. Prison Crystal

This area is one 30-foot-high chamber filled with dim light emanating from the huge crystal at the center of the chamber.
- A soft-glowing purple crystal with a 10-foot diameter and nearly 30-foot height rises out of a dais toward a vaulted ceiling.
- The silhouette of a cloaked figure with ten arms holding a large sword floats at the center of the crystal.
- A spiral staircase next to the crystal rises 15 feet into the air to meet a catwalk that circles the crystal and leads to an iron throne that touches the glowing stone.

The heavy crystal is built into the floor and has 50 Stamina. A creature who touches the crystal or sits in the throne can communicate telepathically with the entity trapped inside. If a crow wants to recall lore about the entity, they can make the following test:

**2d10 + M**
- **≤11 (Tier 1):** No info.
- **12–16 (Tier 2):** They know the info in the first paragraph of The Ring Collector (below).
- **17+ (Tier 3):** They know the info in the first two paragraphs of The Ring Collector (below).

### The Ring Collector
The Ring Collector is a creature gifted with supernatural strength and speed who is obsessed with finding and collecting magic rings. They invaded and wiped out several villages during the Necromancer War in pursuit of rings.

The Ring Collector is Craelin’s advisor, Namlin. Namlin stayed loyal to the archmage even after all others left the necromancer’s side. As a gift, Craelin warped Namlin’s flesh and turned them into a hunter of magic rings, ones meant to eventually adorn the necromancer’s fingers.

Namlin became too covetous of their rings and refused to give them over to Craelin. The necromancer stripped the Ring Collector of their collection and imprisoned them in this crystal for insolence a short time before disappearing.

### Speaking with Namlin
When a PC touches the crystal, they receive a telepathic message from Namlin, asking calmly and softly for freedom and promising rewards. The PC can respond telepathically and have a conversation with Namlin as long as they remain touching the crystal. If asked about their background, Namlin claims to have been betrayed by Craelin and refers to themself as “the Collector” if asked for a name.

Namlin knows much of the library’s secrets and is willing to give information to the PCs in exchange for freedom. They give one of the following details the PCs don’t already know to prove their knowledge, but provide no other information about the library until they’re free:
- How the trap on the locked cabinet works in area 3 and the location of its key in area 6
- The alcove hidden behind the shelf in area 5
- How the trap in area 7 works and which book to select to prevent it from happening
- The properties of a magic item found by the PCs in the library

If the PCs ask about the sword, Namlin promises to give it to them if they are freed and bring them the *minor telekinesis ring* from area 8.

If Namlin is freed and the PCs don’t have the ring, the collector shares any promised secrets and then leaves the chamber, walking directly to area 8, breaking down the door, and moving inside to grab the ring. All blood creatures cower in Namlin’s presence, staying at least 5 squares away and only attacking creatures who attack them first or are more than 5 squares from the collector. The collector takes the *minor telekinesis ring* from area 8 then vanishes from the library.

If the PCs give Namlin the ring after freeing them, the collector gives them a magic **greatsword which has the exploding property** (see *Characters*). If the PCs have the ring and don’t give it to Namlin, Namlin fights until the ring is surrendered. Whenever Namlin gets the ring, they vanish from the library.

## 5. Laboratory

- A large stone table carved with glyphs holds the corpse of a wolf-sized spider, thirteen rotting dead crabs, a bucket of blood, and a journal.
- Empty jars sit on shelves and tables on the walls.
- A spiral staircase leads up.

### Blood Creature Ritual
The journal has extensive notes on a magic ritual to create a blood creature. A PC who studies the journal as a rest activity can attempt to perform the ritual. The ritual requires the special table in this area, the corpses of a giant spider and thirteen crabs, and a bucket filled with blood. Attempting the ritual consumes the materials used in it. The PC performing the ritual must make the following test:

**2d10 + M**
- **≤11 (Tier 1):** The ritual materials are destroyed, and the PC takes 3d6 P dam. If this kills the PC, they become a blood creature C under the Ref’s control.
- **12–16 (Tier 2):** The PC creates a blood creature B that they know will remain under their control until the end of the DT or harmed by the PC or their allies.
- **17+ (Tier 3):** The PC creates a blood creature B that is now a pet they own.

### Shelves and Tables
A PC who searches the tables finds a **smoke bomb**.

A PC who examines the room for secrets can make the following test. If a PC searches the floor in front of the northernmost shelves specifically, they automatically get a tier 3 result on the test:

**2d10 + M**
- **≤11 (Tier 1):** No info.
- **12–16 (Tier 2):** The PC notices scratches on the floor in front of the shelf.
- **17+ (Tier 3):** As tier 2, but the PC also infers this means the shelf is often moved to get at something behind it.

If the shelves are moved, the PCs find an alcove behind it that contains **3 blood creature parts** and **38 gc**.

## 6. Private Quarters

- Rotted timbers and torn cloth litter the floor of this chamber, sitting atop a shallow, wide puddle of blood.
- A small crack in the west wall is just big enough for a person to squeeze into.
- A spiral staircase leads down.

### Difficult Terrain
The floor of this chamber is difficult terrain.

### Blood Puddle
A PC suspicious of the puddle can make this test:

**2d10 + M**
- **≤11 (Tier 1):** No info.
- **12–16 (Tier 2):** The PC knows the blood is related to blood creatures but is unsure how.
- **17+ (Tier 3):** The PC knows the puddle is a sleeping blood creature C.

If a creature enters the room without sneaking or touches the puddle, the puddle—which is an unconscious **blood creature C**—awakens. A creature trying not to wake the blood creature must make the following test to sneak into and out of the room, and also if they interact with the coins or iron bars in the alcove:

**2d10 + A**
- **≤11 (Tier 1):** The creature wakes.
- **12–16 (Tier 2):** The creature remains asleep but stirs. If any PC makes this test and gets this result again, the creature wakes.
- **17+ (Tier 3):** The creature remains asleep.

### Alcove Treasure
The alcove contains a 2-foot-long wood chest that is locked. It can be easily smashed or forced open; doing so wakes the blood creature. A PC can attempt to pick the lock with this test:

**2d10 + A**
- **≤11 (Tier 1):** The chest stays locked. The creature wakes.
- **12–16 (Tier 2):** The chest unlocks, but the noise wakes the creature.
- **17+ (Tier 3):** The chest is unlocked. The creature stays asleep.

The chest contains **5 iron bars (Small crafting materials)**, **19 gc**, and a hand-written note on a piece of parchment that says “Gold clasps.” The chest also contains a **red key** with a head shaped like a blood droplet.

## 7. Forbidden Books

**The door to this chamber from area 2 is locked**, and can be unlocked with the key in area 6. A creature can attempt to pick the lock or break the door down with the following test:

**2d10 + A or S**
- **≤11 (Tier 1):** Door remains locked, and the attempt makes prolonged noise.
- **12–16 (Tier 2):** The door opens, and the attempt makes prolonged noise.
- **17+ (Tier 3):** The door opens quietly.

The door has 50 Stamina. Attacking the door makes prolonged noise. Any prolonged noise draws the attention of the blood creatures in area 3 and also triggers an encounter check.

When the PCs enter the area, share these details:
- A spiral staircase at the center of the room leads from the floor up to a metal trapdoor.
- The flesh-coated walls in this room are covered in green, throbbing pustules.
- The room’s five longest walls each have a leather-bound tome chained to fleshy plinths.
- The books are identical, save for the metal of their clasps, which are, starting on the westmost wall and going clockwise: gold, silver, copper, brass, and platinum.
- A metal drain grate sits beneath the stairs.
- The bleached bones of humanoids litter the floor.

### Acid Pustules
If a creature touches a book other than the one with gold clasps or gets violent with a pustule, a trap triggers. Begin tracking the game in combat rounds.

- At the start of the first round, the door and trapdoor in this room shut and lock (if not destroyed), and neither can be unlocked with the key from area 6 while the trap is active. The trapdoor has the same statistics as the door in this chamber that leads to area 2.
- Also at the start of the first round, the pustules pop and begin spraying constant streams of a flesh-eating acid into the room.
- At the end of the first round, the acid in the room reaches a height of 5 feet.
- The height is 10 feet by the end of the second round, and the entire room is flooded with acid by the end of the third round, which is also when the acid stops streaming.

If a creature starts their turn in or enters the acid, they take 1d6 damage. If they are fully submerged in the acid, they take 2d6 damage instead.

If the door to area 2 is opened, the acid instead flows out into that area, creating a puddle of acid that is 1-foot deep and moves 5 squares west at the end of each round. All acid drains at the end of the DT.

### Books
The gold-clasped book is a ***deadspeech* spellbook**. The other books are each empty journals.

### Blood Creatures

# Dungeon: Floating Manor

The Wolverly noble family remained loyal to Ornassa even after her turn to necromancy. Clara and Freddard Wolverly and their adult children, Acob and Tilly, directed one of Ornassa’s great armies of undead. When the necromancer disappeared, Wolverly Manor, the family’s great home, was raised on a floating hunk of earth into the sky. No word has come from the manor since, but the family’s great treasures remain in the manor, ready for the taking.

> [!note] What the PCs Don't Know
> What the PCs don’t know is that Lisbeth, the Wolverlys’ scullery maid, despised the way the nobles treated the undead like expendable tools. She murdered the Wolverlys and now cares for the undead. The other servants have all been fed to the ravenous monsters.

## Manor Approach

When the PCs reach Wolverly Manor, they notice the following details:
- Above the trees of the Wist Weald, a two-story manor floats in the sky on an island of earth.
- The island is roughly 30 feet tall and floats 10 feet above a clearing below.
- Trees around the clearing grow to a height of 50 feet.

### Getting to the Manor
If the PCs have a way to fly, they can easily reach the manor. They could also tie a rope to a grappling hook, hurl the grappling hook up onto the island and then climb the rope.

If the PCs can reach the bottom of the island, which is 2 squares up from the ground, they can attempt to scale it, or they could climb a tree at the edge of the clearing and leap from it onto the island. Either method requires the following test. Climbing the tree grants a PC an edge on the test thanks to all the branches. If a PC climbs a tree, they must cross a distance of 2 squares of open space to reach the surface of the island:

**2d10 + S**
- **≤11 (Tier 1):** The PC can’t climb.
- **12–16 (Tier 2):** The PC climbs to their destination, but the stress of the climb deals 2 P dam.
- **17+ (Tier 3):** The PC climbs to their destination.

### Outside the Manor
When a PC gets to the island, they notice the following details:
- The manor has windows shuttered from the inside on both stories of the house.
- Several chimneys line the top of the house.
- The ground is devoid of plant life, though a few dead trees and boulders are scattered through the area.

If the PCs are on the north side of the manor, they also notice:
- A small outhouse with a skull carved in the door stands behind the house.
- At the other end of the island, a dirt-covered cart sits on the ground.
- A 10-foot-high, wrought iron fence surrounds a small graveyard, with gravestones surrounding a skull-shaped mausoleum.

> [!important] First Dungeon Turn
> **When the PCs reach the island’s surface, the first Dungeon Turn begins.** Let them know, and start the 30-minute timer.

## Entering the Manor

The PCs can enter the manor in any of the following ways:
- They can enter one of the outside doors to area 1 or 3.
- They can enter the outhouse (area 14).
- They can climb the fence into area 13.
- A creature who can reach the roof can attempt to climb down one of the chimneys to a fireplace (see Shared Details).
- They can enter through a shuttered window. If they bash open a shuttered window, they trigger an encounter check. If they want to open a window from the outside with a knife, they must make the following test:

**2d10 + A**
- **≤11 (Tier 1):** The window remains shut.
- **12–16 (Tier 2):** The window opens, but makes a loud sound, triggering an encounter check.
- **17+ (Tier 3):** The window opens.

### Shared Details

Unless otherwise noted, interior areas of the manor share the following details:
- **Darkness:** The interior areas of the manor have no light sources.
- **Fireplaces:** Each fireplace is connected via a chimney to one above it. A Medium or smaller creature can squeeze through a chimney. Climbing up or down a chimney requires the following test:

**2d10 + S**
- **≤11 (Tier 1):** The climbing creature falls.
- **12–16 (Tier 2):** The creature climbs to the level they wish to enter, but takes 1 P dam from the strain of climbing.
- **17+ (Tier 3):** The creature climbs to their destination.

- **Floor Heights:** The second floor of the manor is 10 feet above the first. The ceiling of the second floor is 10 feet high.
- **Map:** Areas of the manor are keyed to the Floating Manor map.
- **Noticeable Details:** Every area starts with a bulleted list of details the PCs notice when they enter for the first time, provided they can discern the details in the darkness.

## Manor Encounters

The EN for the manor is 9. If an encounter roll occurs during a combat anywhere except for area 15, then the EN is 8. When a random encounter occurs, roll on the Undead Encounters table in *The Ref*.

## 1. Grand Hall

- Five faded and worn tapestries, each 10 feet tall and 15 feet wide and stained with splatters of dried blood, hang from the walls.
- The first floor has doors that lead east and west.
- A staircase on the north end of the room leads up to a landing and another door and then a balcony that wraps around the edges of the room.
- On the balcony, there is one door to the east and one to the west.

### Tapestries
Each tapestry depicts a family of four—a mother, a father, a son, and a daughter—in blood red robes. The individual tapestries show the following details, starting with the northeast tapestry and continuing clockwise around the room:
- **Tapestry 1:** The family kneels at the feet of a woman in black robes. Half her face is just a skull with a glowing red eye. The PCs know this woman is Ornassa.
- **Tapestry 2:** The family walks through a graveyard, supervising the work of servants digging up graves and hauling out corpses.
- **Tapestry 3:** The family performs rituals to turn the corpses into undead monsters.
- **Tapestry 4:** The family standing in front of an army of undead monsters, leading a march.
- **Tapestry 5:** The family seated at a long table, feasting and raising their glasses as the floating half-skull face of Ornassa looks upon them.

Each tapestry is a **Medium art object worth 100 gc**.

### Locked Door
The east door on the second floor of this area is locked. The door can be unlocked with a key held by Lisbeth in area 3.

Bashing down the door triggers an encounter check. A creature attempting to pick the lock can make the following test:

**2d10 + A**
- **≤11 (Tier 1):** The door remains locked.
- **12–16 (Tier 2):** The door is unlocked and the tool used to pick the lock breaks.
- **17+ (Tier 3):** The door is unlocked.

## 3. Kitchen

This area is filled with bright light from the fire burning beneath the pot.
- A warm, bright fire burns beneath a pot, cooking something savory and meaty.
- Five skeletons are stuffed in the southeast corner in front of a large chest.
- Stairs in the northeast corner spiral up.
- A creature with a human torso, a giant slug tail, and compound insect eyes moves about the kitchen deftly with a knife.
- The woman cuts dried meat off the bones of what is mostly a human skeleton.
- *(If the undead haven’t gone to area 7)* A group of undead, crawling on all fours with jagged spikes protruding from their backs, follows her.

### Undead
There is one **undead A** per creature in the PCs’ party. They don’t attack unless the PCs harm them or Lisbeth first or Lisbeth orders it. If fighting occurs in here for longer than 2 rounds, the creatures in area 7 come down the stairs.

### Lisbeth
Lisbeth is the only human left alive. She is a **thief (Power 6)**, with a speed of 4 who is unaffected by dim light and darkness thanks to her transformation. She coordinated the murder of the Wolverlys after they raised the manor into the sky and fed the other servants to the undead, who are her precious pets.

Lisbeth’s mind and soul were corrupted by the magic the Wolverlys used. She tends not to think clearly or logically and can be fooled into accepting the presence of PCs who express loving interest in her pets. She attacks thieves, liars, or anyone harming her or her pets.

If the PCs approach her without malice, she expresses glee that her delivery has finally arrived. She’s been rationing food for years and was expecting more meat to feed her babies. If the PCs claim to have the delivery, she shares the following information if asked about these subjects:
- **The Wolverlys:** Lisbeth proudly admits that she murdered the nobles for their crimes against her precious darlings. She burned Clara and Freddard alive in their sleep, stabbed Acob at the dining room table, and Tilly disappeared into the outhouse and was never seen again.
- **Other Servants:** The other servants were just as mean to the undead as the Wolverlys, and the poor babies need to eat. She motions to the skeletons as she talks about this.
- **The Undead:** Lisbeth mentions the undead are her beautiful babies. She tells the PCs that Horace Wolverly, beloved father of Freddard, was raised by the family to guard the crypt below and is the only undead she doesn’t like. Horace despised and mocked Lisbeth when he was alive. There was another undead that didn’t like her, but she managed to chop that one to bits and store it upstairs in the servant quarters.
- **The Manor:** Lisbeth isn’t sure how the manor floats. She’d prefer it be returned to the ground, but Clara raised it years ago in a room in the east wing of the first floor and the door’s been locked since. Lisbeth doesn’t spend much time in the east wing of the second floor, because she knows the Wolverlys trapped their rooms, and she doesn’t want to risk setting them off.

If the PCs claim they are there to deliver food and don’t get her at least 20 rations worth of food before the end of the DT in which they meet her, Lisbeth and the undead in this room go searching for the PCs on the grounds. If they don’t appear to be delivering food, she figures they are liars and attacks.

If the PCs give Lisbeth at least 20 rations worth of food, she offers them a tour of the manor. She shows them the first and second floor of the west wing and the study (area 4) before leaving them in area 1 and bidding them farewell. She answers questions they have, but doesn’t allow them to remove anything from the house. While she is with the PCs, she can prevent the undead in areas 3, 4, and 7 from attacking them.

**Lisbeth holds a key to the locked door in area 1.**

### Chest
Most of the cutlery and cookware are too rusty and worn to sell. However, the chest in the room contains the bloody, torn clothing of the murdered servants and their belongings. There is a **purse** with **136 gc** and two gold rings, each of which is a **Tiny art object worth 50 gc**.

### Stairs
The stairs lead to area 7.

## 4. Study

- Three long, empty bookshelves line the walls of this study set with two plush chairs for reading by a fireplace.
- A metal pillar holds a book wrapped tightly around it with a padlocked, spiked chain carved with small red runes.
- On the ceiling above the pillar clings a mound of gray flesh.

### Chained Book
The padlock can be unlocked by the red key in area 11, which also disarms the trap associated with the chain and book. A PC can attempt to unlock the chain with the following test:

**2d10 + A**
- **≤11 (Tier 1):** The chain remains locked and the trap triggers.
- **12–16 (Tier 2):** The chain is unlocked, but the undead creature above wakes up.
- **17+ (Tier 3):** The chain is unlocked.

Attempting to break the chain triggers its trap.

If the trap triggers, the undead on the ceiling above wakes up and a spirit chain emerges from the physical chain. The creature who triggered the trap is grabbed by the spirit chain. When a creature grabbed by the chain gets a tier 1 or 2 result on the Break Grab maneuver to end the grab, they take 1d6 dam.

A creature can scratch out the runes on the chain with a tool, disarming the trap and destroying any spirit chains it created. A creature can only attempt this outside of combat and must make the following test:

**2d10 + A**
- **≤11 (Tier 1):** The trap isn’t disarmed and triggers.
- **12–16 (Tier 2):** The trap is disarmed, but the physical chain explodes, dealing 1d6 dam to each creature within 1 square of it.
- **17+ (Tier 3):** The trap is disarmed.

The book is a ***wound closure* spellbook**.

### Undead
The gray flesh on the ceiling is an unconscious **undead C** all curled up. The undead wakes up if any loud noises are made in the room, the trap triggers, or if they take damage or are touched.

A PC who wants to know more about the flesh pile can make the following test:

**2d10 + M**
- **≤11 (Tier 1):** The PC knows nothing new.
- **12–16 (Tier 2):** The PC can tell the flesh pile is a creature but has no further information.
- **17+ (Tier 3):** The PC knows the pile is an undead C.

## 6. Sky Pillar

Both doors that lead to this room are metal and have no keyhole. A tiny indentation above the doorknob is emblazoned with the image of a skeletal dog. Freddard’s *death ring* (in area 12) can unlock the door when pressed to the indentation. Either door can be unlocked via a deadbolt from the inside.

The PCs can break down the door. Doing so triggers an encounter rolled on the Undead Encounters table in *The Ref*.

- A huge metal pillar going from floor to ceiling at the center of the room vibrates with power and crackles with tiny bolts of blue lightning running over it.
- The east wall of this room is painted with the image of the very manor you are inside of rising up into the sky.

### Exposing the Pillar
The metal pillar is actually a magic blue crystal surrounded by a steel cylinder. The steel cylinder has 100 Stamina. Whenever a creature touches the pillar with their body or a metal or otherwise conductive item they carry, they take 1d6 P dam as the cylinder’s lightning shocks them.

A creature examining the cylinder finds a small indentation of a furry dog on its north side. Pressing Clara’s *life ring* (in area 11) to the indentation causes the pillar cylinder to disappear.

### Using the Pillar
Once the cylinder is gone and the crystal pillar is exposed, the PCs notice the pillar is carved with two images, one with a skeletal dog and the other with a furry dog. When a PC touches the pillar, they know that they can will the manor to lower back to the ground (or up into the sky if the manor is on the ground) while touching the pillar, and that there may be risks involved in doing so (unless they wear both the *death ring* and the *life ring*).

When a PC wills the manor to the ground they make the following Mind RR. They gain an edge on the RR if they wear either the *death ring* or the *life ring*. If they wear both, they get an automatic tier 3 result on the test:

**2d10 + M**
- **≤11 (Tier 1):** 10 P dam
- **12–16 (Tier 2):** 5 P dam
- **17+ (Tier 3):** No effect

## 8. Servant’s Quarters

- A total of seven perfectly made bunk beds line the north and south walls of this chamber that stinks of rot.
- At the foot of each bed is a wooden chest with a latch held shut by a wood stick instead of a lock.

### Chests
The smell of rot emanates from the chests in the room. Every chest contains a worthless chamberpot, **2d10 gc**, and a hunk of living, malleable flesh that escapes as soon as the chest is cracked open. Each Medium chunk of flesh has 8 Stamina, a speed of 4, and can’t defend itself on its own.

When two chunks of flesh touch, they form a Large-sized **undead F** with 16 Stamina. This creature attempts to smash chests with flesh remaining inside (and anyone in their way). Every chunk of flesh that joins them increases the creature’s Stamina by 8. When four or more chunks of flesh have joined together, the creature’s size becomes Huge. Once fully assembled with all seven chunks, the undead moves to find Lisbeth (see area 3) and destroy her and any other delicious creatures along the way.

The westmost chest also contains a pearl-handled mirror, which is a **Tiny art object worth 100 gc**. The eastmost chest also contains a small metal box containing a jasper (**gem worth 25 gc**), a turquoise (**gem worth 75 gc**), and a topaz (**gem worth 100 gc**).

## 11. Tilly’s Room

This room is filled with dim light thanks to the magic embers in the fireplace.
- An open, empty wardrobe sits on the west wall.
- Atop a nightstand next to a bed, a journal sits.
- The embers of the fireplace here glow low, arranged around a small stone box.

### Journal
The initials “T.W.” are emblazoned on the journal’s cover. The book is locked, though the flimsy clasp is easily broken or picked (no test required). It contains the following undated entries:

- *“Grandfather Horace has been returned to us. Father is a miracle worker thanks to the gifts of Ornassa. Now I am free to visit my grandfather in the crypts whenever I desire.”*
- *“Raising the manor into the sky seemed to upset Lisbeth. She says she feels cut off from the world. But we can’t go back down there. The people have turned against Ornassa and would tear us apart.”*
- *“Lisbeth is annoying. She’s always calling the undead her ‘babies.’ We made them. They are ours to do with as we please. I can see why grandfather Horace hated her. No wonder she can’t go into the crypt to face him.”*
- *“Lisbeth might be planning a revolt. I can’t let her get my ring or she’ll have access to the weapons. Father has hidden his beyond the seal, but I must keep mine. What if we need the weapons?”*
- *“Last night mother and father died. Murdered in their beds by Lisbeth! She’s taking control of the undead. Acob doesn’t believe me, but I know grandfather will. If I can just make it to the crypts without her notice, I can take back the manor.”*

### Stone Box
A creature can attempt to move the box from the magic embers without disturbing them with the following test:

**2d10 + A**
- **≤11 (Tier 1):** The PC doesn’t grab the box and disturbs the embers.
- **12–16 (Tier 2):** The PC grabs the box and disturbs the embers.
- **17+ (Tier 3):** The PC grabs the box without disturbing the embers.

If the embers around the stone box are disturbed, they explode. Each creature in the room must make an Agility RR:

**2d10 + A**
- **≤11 (Tier 1):** 8 dam
- **12–16 (Tier 2):** 4 dam
- **17+ (Tier 3):** No effect

The box contains the ***life ring*** (a signet ring depicting a furry dog and the initials “C.W.”) and a **red key**.

## 12. Master Bedroom

Dim red light fills this room thanks to the glowing sigil.

- The room is filled with a soft red glow that emanates from a sigil drawn on the floor.

The sigil on the floor has runes arranged around its edge. A creature who has the Magic Lore expertise knows the runes say, *“Speak the name of our crypt’s protector.”*

If a creature touches the sigil and says the name **Horace**, a magic signet ring appears on the ground next to them in a flash of brimstone. It is the ***death ring***, depicting a skeletal dog and the initials “F.W.”

If any other name is spoken while touching the sigil, the sigil summons one or more undead who appear in the room. The undead should be rolled on the Undead Encounters table in *The Ref*.

## 15. Crypt

- Internment niches line the walls of this chamber, bones of dead Wolverlys on shelves with placards stating their names.
- A stone statue of Ornassa, a cloaked elderly woman with half her skull exposed on one side of her face, holds forth a bowl, a steel chest at her feet.
- As you enter this chamber, a female voice rings out from the statue, *“Place an offering to honor the guardian.”*
- An undead warrior emerges from behind the statue, clad in armor and carrying a sword, eyeless sockets searching for a threat.

### Undead
The **undead G** in this chamber is made from the remains of Horace Wolverly. He can’t speak. Sworn to guard the family crypt, Horace follows any PCs he notices. If they don’t approach the statue to place an offering, he points towards it. He attacks if the PCs don’t go toward the statue or leave the crypts after pointing at the offering bowl twice. He also attacks if the PCs attack him first or if he catches them in an act of thievery or vandalism. He pursues enemies out of the crypt, but doesn’t follow them into the manor or off of the island.

If the PCs place either Tilly’s signet ring (from area 14), the *life ring* (from area 11), the *death ring* (from area 12), or the head or corpse of Lisbeth (from area 3) in the offering bowl, Horace is pleased. He indicates the PCs can take the chest from beneath the statue. He doesn’t allow them to take the other chests or treasures from the crypt, first clearly warning PCs to leave the chests alone by pointing his sword at them, and attacking if the warning isn’t heeded.

### Chests
The crypt contains three iron chests, all of which are locked. The chests are heavy and require two Medium creatures using both their hand slots to carry them to be moved.

Each chest is locked and can be unlocked with the key held by the corpse in area 14. Given enough time, a creature can easily and loudly pick or break open the lock. If a creature wishes to pick the lock quickly and without drawing the attention of Horace, they must make the following test:

**2d10 + A**
- **≤11 (Tier 1):** The attempt alerts Horace and fails to pick the lock.
- **12–16 (Tier 2):** The attempt alerts Horace, but the lock is picked.
- **17+ (Tier 3):** Horace isn’t alerted and the lock is picked.

#### Chest Contents
- **Northmost Chest:** Contains a magic **steel flail with the vicious property**, an emerald (**gem worth 250 gc**), and **432 gc**.
- **Statue Chest:** Contains a ***group healing* spellbook**, a ***whistle of dog summoning***, a ***necklace of teeth***, and **506 gc**.
- **Southmost Chest:** Contains two golden chalices (each a **Tiny art object worth 100 gc**), and **1,044 gc**.
